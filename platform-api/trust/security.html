Security

Security & Responsible Operations

AGRO-AI's security approach is designed around protecting customer boundaries, credentials, operational evidence and the systems that support agricultural decisions.

Published September 15, 2026. This page describes control principles and does not claim a certification or audit that has not been issued.

1. Identity and access

Access to customer and production systems should be authenticated and authorized using role, organization, project or service-account context. Privileged access should be limited to legitimate operational need and reviewed proportionate to risk.

2. Tenant isolation

Customer boundaries are intended to be enforced by application and infrastructure authorization before data reaches retrieval or reasoning layers. Model output is never treated as proof that a user was authorized to access the underlying data.

3. Credential management

Passwords, API keys, tokens, signing keys and connector secrets should be stored and processed through appropriate server-side or secret-management controls. Credentials should not be exposed in frontend code, logs, analytics payloads or model context.

4. Encryption

AGRO-AI uses encryption and secure transport controls appropriate to the systems and providers in use. Public statements about encryption scope are kept aligned to verified production configuration rather than assuming every datastore, backup or integration behaves identically.

5. Logging and auditability

Security, authentication, API, configuration and workflow events may be logged to support reliability, metering, investigations and customer assurance. Logs are access-controlled and should avoid unnecessary sensitive content.

6. Secure development and change management

Security-sensitive changes should be reviewed and tested proportionate to their risk. Production changes should be traceable to source changes and deployment evidence where supported. New connectors, model providers and data flows should receive security and governance review before handling sensitive production data.

7. Vulnerability and dependency management

AGRO-AI seeks to identify and remediate security vulnerabilities in its code, infrastructure and dependencies based on severity, exploitability and customer impact. High-risk findings receive priority handling.

8. Incident response

Suspected incidents are triaged, contained, investigated and documented. Confirmed events involving covered customer or personal data are communicated in accordance with applicable law and contractual requirements. Unsuccessful probes and routine security events are distinguished from confirmed breaches.

9. Availability, backup and recovery

Critical services should have backup, recovery and operational procedures appropriate to their role. Recovery objectives and customer commitments are stated contractually only where the underlying architecture and operating process support them.

10. Service providers

Infrastructure and software providers with access to covered data should be selected based on business need and appropriate security, privacy and contractual considerations. Material provider changes may trigger trust-document review.

11. Customer responsibilities

Customers should protect credentials, configure access appropriately, promptly revoke unused access, validate high-consequence recommendations and notify AGRO-AI of suspected compromise or vulnerabilities affecting the service.

12. Assurance and certifications

AGRO-AI does not claim independent assurance or security certifications unless they have actually been obtained and the claim accurately reflects scope and validity. Security questionnaires, contractual security terms and current assurance materials may be provided to qualified enterprise customers where appropriate.

13. Reporting security concerns

Security concerns may be sent to contact@agroai-pilot.com with “Security” in the subject line.
