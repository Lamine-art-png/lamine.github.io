Agricultural data governance

Govern the full data lifecycle.

AGRO-AI treats agricultural operational data as a governed asset from authorization and collection through retrieval, intelligence, sharing, retention, export and deletion. This standard explains the public-facing rules behind that program.

1. Objective

AGRO-AI's data governance program is designed to prevent data handling from becoming a series of disconnected engineering, connector, analytics and model decisions. The standard applies to personal information, Customer Data, agricultural operational data, service telemetry, derived data, model inputs/outputs and material business records processed through AGRO-AI systems.

Important distinction: a field boundary, irrigation record or yield history can be commercially sensitive even when it contains no personal information. AGRO-AI therefore applies operational-data governance in addition to privacy-law controls.

2. The governance rule

No material new data flow should enter production without a known owner, authority, purpose, classification, source, destination, access model, third-party path, retention/deletion rule, export/return behavior and incident owner. If AI is involved, the model/provider use must also be known.

This requirement applies especially to new connectors, model providers, analytics tools, support tools, cloud storage, enrichment sources, cross-customer datasets and new write-back or physical-actuation capabilities.

4. Accountability

Data governance requires named responsibility. In a smaller company, one person may hold several roles, but the responsibilities cannot disappear.

Executive owner

Accountable for the governance program and material risk acceptance.

System owner

Accountable for inventory, controls and lifecycle behavior of a system.

Data steward

Accountable for definitions, quality, purpose and handling rules.

Engineering & Security

Implements access, isolation, secrets, logging, backups, deletion and incident controls.

Product

Prevents features from silently creating undisclosed secondary uses.

Legal / Privacy

Reviews roles, notices, DPAs, rights, transfers, vendors and legal change.

5. The registers that make governance real

AGRO-AI's standard calls for living operational records rather than one-time policy documents:

  1. Data Processing Register — purpose, categories, source, system, recipients, geography, authority, retention, deletion and owner.
  2. Data Flow Map — material flows among customers, AGRO-AI, models, storage, analytics, billing, support and integrations.
  3. Subprocessor & Vendor Register — service, data, location, contract/DPA status, security review, owner and review date.
  4. Retention Schedule — category/system-specific retention and backup behavior.
  5. Access Register — privileged roles, approvals, reviews and revocation.
  6. AI / Model Register — provider/model, data sent, training/retention settings, risk tier, evaluation and owner.
  7. Trust Claims Register — every material public trust claim mapped to control and evidence.
  8. Incident Register — incidents, notification decisions, remediation and lessons learned.
  9. Rights / Customer Request Register — access, export, deletion and privacy requests with completion evidence.

6. Data minimization and purpose limitation

Systems should collect and expose only data reasonably necessary for the approved product or operational purpose. Convenience alone is not enough to justify bulk access to a customer's entire drive, database, connector account or model context.

A material secondary use must be reviewed against the original customer authorization, contract, notice and applicable law. Data that was connected to solve one operational problem does not automatically become available for unrelated commercial use.

7. Access and tenant isolation

  • Protected-resource access decisions should be server-authoritative.
  • Tenant/workspace scope is enforced before retrieval and context assembly.
  • Privileged human access should be limited, attributable, approved and reviewable.
  • Credentials and secrets are kept outside normal model context and ordinary logs.
  • Service accounts and API credentials should be scope-limited and revocable.
  • Access should be removed when it is no longer required.

Tenant isolation is a security boundary, not a UI convention. A model or downstream workflow should never be asked to sort out authorization after data from multiple customers has already been assembled.

8. Integration governance

Every production connector should have documented provider/API, requested scopes, data imported or exported, write authority, customer authorization flow, secret storage, webhook/security controls, revocation behavior, downstream model use and retention after disconnection.

The default is least privilege. Read access does not imply write authority. A customer's decision to connect one system does not authorize unrelated connectors or secondary data uses.

9. AI governance

Model-provider adoption is a data-governance event, not only an engineering decision. AGRO-AI reviews what data is sent, why it is sent, whether it is retained, whether it can be used for training, how tenant boundaries are enforced and what human review is required.

Cross-customer training or aggregation requires a separately approved purpose and safeguards. High-consequence workflows require risk-appropriate authorization, provenance and human control. See the AI Data Use Policy.

10. Retention, return, portability and deletion

“As long as necessary” is a legal principle, not an operational schedule. Material data categories should have system-specific retention and deletion behavior that can actually be implemented and tested.

Deletion workflows should account for active records, derived indexes, object/media storage, connector copies, logs, backups, legal holds and downstream processors. Customer exports should be secure, attributable and usable, favoring common machine-readable formats where practical.

AGRO-AI does not promise deletion behavior that the underlying infrastructure cannot support. Where backups require time to age out, public and contractual language should describe that honestly.

11. Vendors, subprocessors and external systems

A provider that receives Customer Data, personal data, credentials or Restricted Operational Data requires review appropriate to risk before production use. Review should cover data-use/training terms, security, incident obligations, confidentiality, retention, deletion, location/transfers, downstream subprocessors and contractual protection.

Customer-authorized agricultural integrations are separately governed because the customer may directly control that third-party relationship. See Subprocessors & Integrations.

12. Security and privacy incidents

AGRO-AI's governance standard requires a documented process for detection, containment, evidence preservation, severity assessment, escalation, customer/legal analysis, notification, recovery and remediation. Notification commitments must match the organization's ability to detect and respond rather than serving as marketing language.

13. Privacy and data impact review

A documented review is expected before a material feature introduces:

  • a new sensitive data category;
  • precise location, imagery or voice at materially greater scale;
  • automated consequential decisioning;
  • a new country or material cross-border data flow;
  • a new model-training use;
  • biometric or similarly high-risk data;
  • a new third-party disclosure;
  • new physical actuation or write-back authority; or
  • material repurposing of existing Customer Data.

14. Trust Claims Register

AGRO-AI's external trust language should be governed just like code. A material statement about security, privacy, data ownership, residency, retention, deletion, certification, AI training or breach notification should have a supporting internal record.

That record should contain the exact claim, scope, exceptions, control, evidence, owner, last test, next review and approval status. If AGRO-AI cannot support the claim, the claim should not be published or used in enterprise procurement responses.

15. Review cadence and assurance

  • Critical vendor/model/control changes: before production change.
  • Privileged access: regular review, with prompt revocation when no longer needed.
  • Trust Claims Register: quarterly and before major procurement responses.
  • Subprocessor registry: on material change and periodic reconciliation.
  • Retention schedule: at least annually and when architecture changes.
  • Full governance program: at least annually.
  • Incidents and near misses: post-event review.

Frameworks are not badges. NIST and ISO frameworks can guide the program, but AGRO-AI will not represent itself as certified, audited or fully compliant merely because controls are mapped to a framework.
