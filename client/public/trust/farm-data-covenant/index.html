Farm Data Trust Covenant

Your data. Your operation. Your control.

Agricultural data can reveal where an operation works, what it grows, how equipment performs, how water is used, what happened in a field and how a business is managed. AGRO-AI treats customer operational data as a protected business asset whether or not a privacy law calls it personal data.

Covenant version 2026-09 · Adopted September 14, 2026

Intelligence should not require surrendering control.

This Covenant is AGRO-AI's plain-language customer-data commitment. It supplements, and does not replace, the applicable contract, Privacy Notice or Data Processing Addendum. Where a signed agreement provides stronger customer rights, that agreement controls.

1

Customer control comes first.

Customers retain all right, title and interest they have in data they provide to AGRO-AI or authorize AGRO-AI to retrieve. AGRO-AI receives only the rights needed to provide, secure, support and operate the authorized services, plus uses the customer separately authorizes.

Connecting a data source does not grant AGRO-AI unrestricted reuse rights.

2

Purpose must be clear.

Customer Data is collected and used for disclosed, authorized purposes. A materially different secondary use requires review against the customer's authorization, agreement, applicable notice and law, and requires additional authorization where those sources require it.

3

We do not sell customer operational data.

AGRO-AI's policy is not to sell or rent Customer Operational Data or use it for third-party targeted advertising. A service provider processing data for AGRO-AI does not receive the right to turn customer farm data into its own advertising inventory.

4

No hidden shared-model training.

Customer Data is not used to train shared AGRO-AI models or third-party foundation models for unrelated customers unless the customer expressly authorizes that use through an appropriate agreement or control. A customer can use AGRO-AI intelligence without silently contributing raw farm records to a cross-customer training pool.

5

Tenant boundaries come before intelligence.

Customer authorization and organization/workspace scope are enforced before data is retrieved for intelligence. Credentials, API keys and connector secrets are not ordinary model context. A model is never treated as the authorization layer.

6

Restricted Operational Data receives heightened protection.

Precise field boundaries and geolocation, farm financial/commercial records, yield and production records, irrigation/controller data, equipment telemetry, non-public imagery, sensitive agronomic records, credentials and equivalent information are treated as Restricted Operational Data unless the customer intentionally makes them public.

These data require tenant scoping, least privilege, controlled vendor paths and purpose-limited handling appropriate to the service.

7

Connections remain customer-authorized.

Third-party data connections are initiated or authorized by the customer or an authorized administrator. Scopes should be limited to what the integration needs. Revoking a connection stops future access under that authorization, subject to the provider's behavior and lawful retention of data already received.

8

Customers can take eligible data with them.

AGRO-AI supports practical export/return processes for eligible Customer Data, subject to security, third-party rights, system constraints and the applicable agreement. Where practical, exports should use common machine-readable formats rather than deliberately locking a customer into a proprietary format.

9

Deletion must be real and explained.

Customers have a process to request deletion or return of eligible Customer Data. Material exceptions—such as legal holds, security records, financial records, active disputes or backup-retention windows—must be explained honestly. Public deletion claims must match production behavior.

10

Material providers should be visible.

AGRO-AI maintains a public transparency page for core subprocessors/infrastructure providers and a governance process for provider changes. Customer-authorized integrations are identified separately because the customer may have a direct relationship with those systems.

11

Aggregation and de-identification require safeguards.

Removing a customer name is not enough if field geometry, location, imagery, equipment identifiers or production patterns can still reveal an operation. Aggregated or de-identified data use requires an approach appropriate to the re-identification risk and intended purpose.

AGRO-AI will not attempt to re-identify data it has committed to maintain as de-identified except for legitimate security testing or as required by law and subject to appropriate controls.

12

Provenance matters.

Where the workflow requires it, AGRO-AI preserves meaningful distinctions among source data, reported observations, measured data, estimates, derived data, model-generated output, human edits and approved actions. Intelligence should make evidence easier to use without erasing where it came from.

13

People remain in control of consequential action.

High-consequence agronomic, water, compliance, financial or physical-execution workflows require risk-appropriate authorization, confidence handling, evidence and human-review controls. Uncertain model output is not represented as verified fact, and a recommendation alone does not grant physical actuation authority.

14

Security is an operating discipline.

AGRO-AI's security program addresses access control, credential protection, tenant isolation, logging, incident response, vulnerability management, change control, backups/recovery and vendor/workforce confidentiality appropriate to the services in use.

We do not claim certifications or independent audit results that have not been issued.

15

Legal requests are handled narrowly.

Where legally permitted, AGRO-AI requires valid legal authority, reviews requests for scope and authority, discloses only what is legally required and notifies the affected customer when permitted by law and contract. Informal interest in a customer's farm data is not treated as valid legal process.

16

Material changes should not be hidden.

Material changes to customer-data commitments are versioned and dated. Where a contract or law requires direct notice, additional authorization or an objection process, that process controls.

17

Trust should be auditable.

AGRO-AI maintains a Trust Claims Register standard that maps material public security, privacy, retention, deletion, AI-training and compliance statements to supporting controls and evidence. If a public claim can no longer be supported, it should be corrected rather than quietly left in place.

How this Covenant fits with legal terms

The Covenant is designed to make AGRO-AI's posture understandable without forcing customers to decode legal language. The Privacy Notice governs personal-information disclosures. The Data Governance Standard explains lifecycle controls. The AI Data Use Policy explains model handling. Enterprise agreements and DPAs establish binding customer-specific obligations.
