What we protect

Operational farm data deserves protection even when privacy law does not call it personal data.

AGRO-AI treats sensitive agricultural operational information as a governed category of its own. Exact contractual treatment depends on the service and agreement, but the classification below drives our design and review process.

Lifecycle

Governance follows the data, not just the database.

A trustworthy system needs controls from collection through deletion. AGRO-AI's governance standard applies across each material stage of the data lifecycle.

1Authorize

Define the customer, organization, source, purpose and permitted access.

2Classify

Identify sensitivity, personal-data status, operational restrictions and retention needs.

3Use

Retrieve only within authorized tenant and workflow boundaries, including AI context assembly.

4Trace

Preserve relevant source, provenance, audit, model and workflow context where the product supports it.

5Return / delete

Support export, account lifecycle and deletion processes subject to lawful retention, security and backup constraints.

Our model-training boundary is intentionally strict.

AGRO-AI's policy is that Customer Data is not used to train shared AGRO-AI models or third-party foundation models for unrelated customers unless the customer has expressly authorized that use through an appropriate agreement or control. Customer data may still be processed for inference, retrieval, support, security, evaluation or service improvement where authorized and subject to the applicable product terms and safeguards.

  • Connector secrets and credentials must not be placed in model context.
  • Tenant isolation applies before retrieval and context assembly.
  • Third-party model-provider use must be reviewed for data handling, retention and training terms.
  • Consequential operational actions remain authorization- and approval-bound.
Transparency

We separate implemented controls from future assurance goals.

What we will state

  • The controls and product boundaries we can support with architecture, configuration, contracts or operating evidence.
  • Which data categories and purposes are relevant to a service.
  • When a customer-authorized integration or subprocessor is involved.
  • Where a policy is a governance requirement rather than a certification.

What we will not pretend

  • We will not display a SOC 2 or ISO certification unless it has actually been issued and is in scope.
  • We will not promise instant deletion from every backup if the infrastructure does not support that statement.
  • We will not use "zero retention," "GDPR compliant" or similar blanket language without the necessary scope and evidence.
  • We will not describe an AI output as a legal, agronomic or regulatory verdict when human review is required.
